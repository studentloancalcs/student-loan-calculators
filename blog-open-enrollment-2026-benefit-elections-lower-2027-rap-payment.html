2026 Open Enrollment Starts in Two Weeks: Which Benefit Elections Actually Lower Your 2027 RAP Payment

Open enrollment windows at most private employers run November 1 through mid-December. For federal student loan borrowers on RAP or IBR, this is the single highest-leverage planning window of the year — every pre-tax dollar you elect this fall shows up as a lower W-2 Box 1 next February, flows into a lower 2026 AGI on the return you file in April, and drives a lower monthly payment at your 2027 recertification. Here is the full map, dollar-for-dollar, with the confirmed 2027 limits and the two elections that look like AGI reducers but are not.

A typical federal student loan borrower in 2026 earning $70,000 pays roughly $360 a month under RAP. The same borrower, after a well-planned open enrollment this fall, can walk into 2027 with pre-tax elections that bring their AGI down by $12,000 to $20,000 and their monthly RAP payment down by $100 to $167 — without taking a pay cut, without changing jobs, and without any one-time financial maneuver. The mechanism is already baked into the way the Department of Education calculates income-driven payments: the servicer pulls the AGI line from the IRS Data Retrieval Tool, and every pre-tax payroll deduction shows up below that line.

The problem is that open enrollment is positioned by most HR departments as a health insurance decision — medical plan, dental, vision, maybe life insurance. The RAP and IBR implications are invisible unless you know to look for them, and they compound in both directions. A borrower who picks the richer PPO plan to avoid a few hundred dollars of out-of-pocket medical risk simultaneously forfeits the HSA, which was the single largest AGI reduction available on the enrollment form. A borrower who defaults to a Roth 401(k) because “Roth is better for young people” locks in a higher 2026 AGI than they had to. These decisions get made in the twenty minutes between a Teams reminder and the enrollment deadline, and then they drive twelve months of student loan payments.

The RAP Formula and Why Open Enrollment Matters

The 2026 Repayment Assistance Plan monthly payment is calculated on a tiered schedule: 1% to 10% of AGI minus a dependent deduction, divided by twelve, with a $10 minimum and a 50% principal match for the first year. The AGI the servicer uses is the one reported on your most recent federal tax return, pulled through the IRS Data Retrieval Tool. For borrowers who enrolled in RAP in July through September 2026, the current payment uses either the 2024 or 2025 return; recertification happens annually on the enrollment anniversary.

The practical implication is a twelve-month lag. Pre-tax elections made during the November 2026 open enrollment lower your 2026 W-2 Box 1 wages. You file your 2026 return in early 2027. The 2026 return then becomes the basis for your 2027 recertification. If you enrolled in RAP in September 2026, your first recertification lands in September 2027. The AGI the servicer pulls at that recertification is the 2026 AGI you built this fall. The 2027 Pre-Tax Limits Confirmed by the IRS

Before deciding how much to elect, know the ceilings for the plan year. The IRS confirmed 2027 HSA limits in Revenue Procedure 2026-23 in May; the 2027 retirement plan limits are projected to final by late October 2026 (with the formal announcement typically coming the first week of November).

2027 Contribution Limits (Open Enrollment Planning)

HSA, self-only HDHP: $4,500 (up from $4,400 in 2026).

HSA, family HDHP: $9,000 (up from $8,750 in 2026).

HSA, age-55 catch-up: $1,000 (unchanged).

401(k) and 403(b) elective deferral: projected $25,000 (up from $24,500 in 2026).

401(k) age-50 catch-up: projected $8,000.

401(k) age 60–63 super catch-up (SECURE 2.0): projected $12,000.

Traditional and Roth IRA: projected $7,500 (up from $7,000 in 2026).

Health FSA: projected $3,400 (up from $3,300 in 2026).

Dependent Care FSA: $5,000 (statutory limit, unchanged).

Qualified transportation (commuter) benefit: projected $335 per month for transit and parking each.

The retirement plan projections are based on third-quarter 2026 CPI data and are expected to be confirmed when the IRS publishes Notice 2026-XX in late October or early November. Enrollment forms typically let you set the contribution as a percentage of pay or a flat dollar amount; the percentage election adjusts automatically if the ceiling moves, which is the safer choice for a borrower who wants to max.

The AGI-Reduction Hierarchy for Open Enrollment

Not every pre-tax election has the same effect on your RAP payment. Rank them by AGI leverage per dollar elected, and the enrollment decisions almost make themselves.

Tier 1: Full AGI reduction, no asterisks. HSA through payroll (requires HDHP coverage), traditional 401(k) and 403(b), SIMPLE IRA through payroll, 457(b) governmental and non-governmental. Every dollar elected comes out of W-2 Box 1 wages, drops AGI by the same dollar, and drops the RAP monthly payment by roughly the AGI change times 10% divided by 12.

Tier 2: Full AGI reduction, with eligibility strings. Dependent Care FSA (up to $5,000) requires a qualifying dependent and qualifying care expenses. Health FSA (up to $3,400 projected) reduces AGI but disqualifies you from HSA contributions unless the FSA is a limited-purpose FSA (dental and vision only). Commuter benefits (transit, parking) are pre-tax up to the monthly limit but only usable for the specific expense category.

Tier 3: Partial or indirect AGI reduction. Group term life insurance premiums above $50,000 of coverage are imputed as income (so declining employer-paid coverage above $50,000 can slightly lower AGI, though this is rare). Pre-tax long-term disability premiums reduce AGI slightly but make future disability benefits taxable. Adoption assistance and tuition reimbursement up to the statutory caps are excluded from income but are usually not elective during open enrollment.

Tier 4: No AGI reduction, often mistaken for one. Roth 401(k) and Roth 403(b) contributions are after-tax; they appear in W-2 Box 1 and do not reduce AGI. After-tax 401(k) contributions (the mega-backdoor Roth lever) similarly do not reduce AGI. Voluntary benefits like accident, hospital indemnity, and critical illness insurance paid through payroll are typically after-tax. Employee-paid short-term disability premiums are usually after-tax (which keeps the benefits tax-free).

The HSA-Versus-Health-FSA Decision for RAP Borrowers

The single most consequential open enrollment choice for a RAP borrower is HSA versus health FSA. On paper they look similar — pre-tax dollars for medical expenses — but the rules diverge in ways that matter for AGI reduction.

The HSA is funded through payroll (W-2 Box 1 reduction) or made deductible on Schedule 1 line 13 if contributed outside payroll. The 2027 limit is $4,500 for single HDHP coverage and $9,000 for family HDHP coverage. HSA balances roll over indefinitely, follow you between employers, and can be invested after a plan-set threshold. The hitch: you must be enrolled in an HSA-qualified HDHP for the plan year, with no disqualifying coverage (general-purpose health FSA, Medicare, VA care within 90 days).

The health FSA is funded through payroll (same W-2 Box 1 reduction), capped at the projected $3,400 for 2027, and is use-it-or-lose-it with a limited $660 carryover or 2.5-month grace period depending on the plan. It is available with any medical plan (PPO, HMO, HDHP) and does not require HSA-ineligible coverage. The hitch: a general-purpose health FSA makes you ineligible for HSA contributions.

For a RAP borrower weighing the two, the question is almost always resolved in favor of the HSA plus a limited-purpose FSA for dental and vision. The HSA ceiling is higher ($4,500 vs. $3,400), the money rolls over, and the HDHP premium is usually lower than the PPO premium, which frees cash flow to fund the HSA toward the ceiling. For borrowers with meaningful ongoing prescription or chronic care costs, the PPO plus general-purpose FSA may still win on total out-of-pocket, but the AGI effect of the FSA alone is smaller than the HSA alone. The Plan Comparison tool handles the RAP side of the trade; a side-by-side medical cost model from the HR portal handles the medical side.

Worked Example 1: Single Borrower at $55,000 AGI

Megan, 28, is a graphic designer at a mid-sized agency. She enrolled in RAP in August 2026 with a $55,000 AGI from her 2024 return, which produces a monthly payment of roughly $225 after the $10,500 single-filer deduction. Her current open enrollment choices offer: PPO vs. HDHP medical, Roth or traditional 401(k) at 6% employer match, dependent care FSA (not applicable — no children), transit benefit, and voluntary life insurance.

Megan’s Pre-Tax Elections for 2027

HSA (via HDHP): $4,500 (full single limit)

Traditional 401(k): $6,600 (12% of pay to capture full 6% match)

Transit benefit: $2,400 ($200/month, pre-tax commute)

Total pre-tax reduction: $13,500

Projected 2026 AGI: $55,000 − $13,500 = $41,500

Projected 2027 RAP monthly: roughly $113 (down $112/month from $225)

Megan’s annual RAP savings at the new rate: $1,344. Her net additional savings into the HSA and 401(k): $11,100 (the $2,400 transit covers an expense she already has). Her take-home-pay reduction from the shift: approximately $4,100, because the pre-tax contributions also reduce federal income tax (22% bracket), FICA on the HSA, and state tax (varies). The 401(k) match adds roughly $3,300 to retirement savings. Total first-year benefit of the open enrollment move: $12,500 in deferred wealth plus $1,344 in RAP cash flow, for a $4,100 take-home-pay reduction.

Worked Example 2: Married Household at $95,000 Combined AGI

David and Jenna, both 34, both have student loans. David is a nurse at a hospital system with a 403(b) and HSA; Jenna is a graphic designer at a small firm with a 401(k) and no HSA (PPO only). Combined 2024 AGI: $95,000. Both are on RAP, enrolled in September 2026. Current combined RAP monthly: roughly $548 after the $21,000 joint-filer deduction and no dependent deduction.

David and Jenna’s Pre-Tax Elections for 2027

David’s HSA (via family HDHP, Jenna joins his plan): $9,000 (full family limit)

David’s 403(b) traditional: $10,000

Jenna’s 401(k) traditional: $5,000 (6% match)

David’s transit benefit: $3,600

Total pre-tax reduction: $27,600

Projected 2026 AGI: $95,000 − $27,600 = $67,400

Projected 2027 RAP monthly: roughly $320 (down $228/month from $548)

The family HDHP switch is the mechanical pivot: Jenna moving from her employer’s PPO to David’s HDHP unlocks the full $9,000 family HSA ceiling. If both of them stay on separate self-only HDHP coverage, their combined HSA limit is still $9,000 ($4,500 each, aggregated). The combined RAP savings at the new payment: $2,736 annually, with the trade-off being the HDHP deductible exposure (which the HSA balance offsets within two or three years of full-ceiling contributions). Three Open Enrollment Traps That Cost RAP Borrowers Money

Trap 1: Defaulting to Roth 401(k) because the HR orientation deck said “Roth is better when you’re young.” That orientation deck does not know you are on RAP. Roth contributions do not reduce AGI, so they do not reduce the RAP monthly payment. For a borrower pursuing PSLF, the Roth decision costs twice: once in the current RAP payment and once in the forgivable balance at year ten. If your household is below the IRA phase-out (modified AGI under $89,000 single, $146,000 married joint for 2027), the backdoor-equivalent is to contribute traditional to the 401(k) now and move to Roth conversions later when your income strategy allows it.

Trap 2: Picking the general-purpose health FSA while also wanting to fund an HSA. A general-purpose health FSA (available for any medical expense) disqualifies you from HSA contributions for the entire plan year, under the all-or-nothing HSA eligibility rules. If your employer offers both, the compatible pairing is HDHP + HSA + limited-purpose FSA (dental and vision only). If your employer offers only a general-purpose FSA alongside an HDHP, electing the FSA kills the HSA; skip the FSA.

Trap 3: Over-funding the dependent care FSA to the $5,000 statutory limit without actually having $5,000 of qualifying expenses. The dependent care FSA is use-it-or-lose-it (no carryover and no grace period at most plans). An unused balance forfeits. The W-2 reduction still happens, so AGI is reduced for RAP, but the forfeited balance is money you paid for and did not use. For borrowers with full-time daycare for a child under 13, the full $5,000 is almost always consumable. For borrowers with occasional summer camp or after-school care, elect to the expected expense level, not the ceiling.

Timing: What to Do When Your Open Enrollment Opens

Two weeks before enrollment opens. Pull your current year-to-date W-2 (from your October paycheck). Note the Box 1 wages, Box 12 codes (D for 401(k), W for HSA), and estimate your full-year 2026 AGI from the YTD run rate. This is the baseline the servicer will use at your first recertification.

Enrollment week. Model two scenarios in the RAP Calculator: your current AGI with no changes, and your current AGI minus the pre-tax elections you’re considering. The monthly payment delta is what you’re trading your take-home-pay reduction against.

During enrollment. Prefer percentage-of-pay elections for 401(k)/403(b) over flat dollar amounts, so the election rides any 2027 limit increase without a re-election. Fund HSA to the ceiling if cash flow allows; dollar-for-dollar it has the highest AGI leverage of any available election because of the triple tax advantage (no federal income tax, no FICA on payroll contributions, no state tax in most states, tax-free growth, tax-free qualified withdrawals).

After enrollment. Save a PDF of the confirmation page. In January 2027, check your first paycheck to verify the elections took effect at the amounts you set. If a deduction is missing or wrong, HR’s correction window typically closes 30 to 60 days after the plan year begins. Election errors caught in late January are usually fixable; errors caught in April are usually locked until the next open enrollment or a qualifying life event.

PSLF Borrowers: Open Enrollment Has a Compounding Return

For borrowers pursuing Public Service Loan Forgiveness, every dollar of AGI reduction has a stacked payoff: a lower current RAP payment plus a larger balance forgiven at month 120. A PSLF-track borrower earning $85,000 who moves from $0 pre-tax to the elections in the Megan example reduces their 2027 RAP monthly by roughly $112 and, over the ten-year PSLF window, increases cumulative forgiven balance by approximately $13,400 (the compounded difference in payment amounts across the remaining qualifying months, adjusted for annual recertification and standard-plan income growth assumptions). The PSLF tracker shows the forgiveness math at specific payment levels.

The compounding is why PSLF borrowers should treat open enrollment with more care than non-PSLF borrowers. The non-PSLF borrower is trading current cash flow for pre-tax savings; the PSLF borrower is doing that plus turning taxable future dollars into forgiven loan balance. The HSA, traditional 401(k), and limited-purpose FSA pairing is the standard PSLF-track open enrollment stack.

What If I Just Switched From SAVE to RAP?

Borrowers who switched from SAVE to RAP during the summer 2026 transition window are a specific sub-case. The RAP enrollment reset the recertification clock, so a July 2026 enrollee’s first annual recertification is July 2027, driven by the 2026 AGI you build this fall. If you were on SAVE and your prior IDR plan used a 2023 or 2024 AGI, the 2026 return will be the first full year the servicer sees under the RAP rules. Making the elections count this fall is especially important for SAVE-to-RAP borrowers whose 2024 or 2025 AGI was unrepresentatively high (overtime year, bonus year, side-hustle windfall).

The Open Enrollment Checklist for Student Loan Borrowers

1. Confirm your current RAP AGI by logging into StudentAid.gov and checking the Payment Breakdown page.

2. Estimate your 2026 projected AGI from your most recent pay stub (YTD Box 1 wages scaled to 12 months).

3. List every pre-tax election available on your enrollment form. Separate them into Tier 1 (full AGI reduction) and Tier 4 (no AGI reduction).

4. If medical plan options include an HSA-qualified HDHP, run the premium math and the HSA max against the PPO alternative. Default to HDHP+HSA unless you have strong clinical reasons to pick PPO.

5. Set 401(k) or 403(b) to the full match percentage at minimum (traditional, not Roth, for RAP optimization). Stretch toward the $25,000 projected 2027 limit as cash flow allows.

6. Elect transit benefits only up to your actual commute cost. Over-electing forfeits the balance (though some plans allow carryover).

7. Elect dependent care FSA only up to actual expected expenses. Over-electing forfeits the balance.

8. Save your enrollment confirmation PDF. Verify first-paycheck accuracy in January 2027.

Bottom Line

Open enrollment is not a benefits task. For federal student loan borrowers on RAP or IBR, it is the single highest-leverage AGI-planning window on the calendar. Every pre-tax dollar you elect in November and December lowers W-2 Box 1 wages, which lowers 2026 AGI, which lowers the 2027 recertification payment the servicer will pull from the IRS twelve months from now. The elections that count — HSA, traditional 401(k) or 403(b), dependent care FSA, commuter benefits — are standard on almost every private employer’s enrollment form. The elections that do not count — Roth 401(k), voluntary life, accident riders — are often marketed harder because they generate commissions, not payment reductions.

Twenty minutes of modeling in October produces twelve months of lower RAP payments starting in late 2027. For a PSLF-track borrower, those twenty minutes also increase the forgivable balance at month 120 by tens of thousands of dollars.
