Student Loan Rehabilitation November 2026: Start the 9-Month Payment Stream This Week to Exit Default Before August 2027 Garnishment Peak

Federal student loan rehabilitation requires 9 voluntary on-time monthly payments inside a 10-consecutive-month window. A defaulted borrower who starts the stream the week of November 11, 2026 completes it in August 2027 — before the Department of Education's second scheduled administrative wage garnishment wave reaches employer mailrooms in September 2027. This guide walks the 15 percent of discretionary income formula, the reasonable-and-affordable alternative with a $5 floor, the written request template that produces the lowest payment, the rehabilitation vs consolidation decision for borrowers who want to exit default faster, what rehabilitation removes from the credit report, and the three timing mistakes that reset the 9-month clock.

Rehabilitation is the oldest and most underused tool for exiting federal student loan default. The Department of Education's own November 2026 collections dashboard shows roughly 5.4 million federal student loan borrowers in default status, and fewer than 11 percent of them had an active rehabilitation agreement on file as of the most recent monthly report. The gap is not awareness. Most defaulted borrowers have heard of rehabilitation. The gap is paperwork, payment-amount confusion, and the widespread belief that the monthly payment is unaffordable because servicers quote the 15 percent of discretionary income figure first instead of the reasonable-and-affordable alternative that most borrowers actually qualify for.

The calendar is the reason to act in the week of November 11, 2026. Rehabilitation requires 9 voluntary monthly payments. If the first payment lands between November 11 and November 20, 2026, the ninth payment lands in July or August 2027, and the loan exits default in the same month. The Department of Education's administrative wage garnishment wave schedule, published in July 2026 and updated in October 2026, sent 65-day pre-garnishment notices in staggered batches through the second half of 2026 and projects a second wave of actual AWG orders landing with employers in September and October 2027. A rehabilitation completed in August 2027 ends collection activity before any second-wave AWG order can arrive. A rehabilitation that starts in February 2027 completes in November 2027, with three months of garnished wages in between. How the 9-Month Clock Actually Works

The statute at 34 CFR 685.211(f) requires 9 "voluntary, on-time, full, monthly payments" made within a 10-consecutive-month period. Each piece of that sentence is doing specific work. Voluntary means the payment originates from the borrower rather than from an involuntary collection method; a payment seized through administrative wage garnishment or tax refund offset does not count toward the 9, which is why a borrower whose wages are being garnished must still write a separate check to start the rehabilitation stream. On-time means received within 20 days of the scheduled due date; a payment received between day 21 and day 30 may be flagged as late, and a payment received after day 30 is treated as missed. Full means the agreed-upon rehabilitation amount, not a partial payment. Monthly means one payment per calendar month, not weekly or biweekly payments that happen to add up to the monthly amount; a borrower who sends two $100 payments in a single month gets credit for one month, not two.

The 10-consecutive-month window is the stress point. The 9 payments must fall inside a 10-month calendar run, which means a borrower can miss at most one month before the clock resets. A borrower who misses two months inside the window has failed that rehabilitation attempt and must restart from the beginning with a new agreement. The practical defense against an accidental miss is to set up ACH autopay for the rehabilitation payment at the start of the stream. Autopay on a defaulted loan does not trigger the standard 0.25 percent interest rate reduction (that reduction requires the loan to be in good standing), but it does guarantee the 9-payment stream completes without a missed-payment reset. Most servicers will set up rehabilitation autopay by phone during the same call that establishes the payment amount.

The 2026 Payment Formula and the Reasonable-and-Affordable Alternative

The default statutory formula for the rehabilitation payment is 15 percent of the borrower's annual discretionary income divided by 12. Discretionary income here uses the older IDR definition: the amount by which the borrower's adjusted gross income exceeds 150 percent of the federal poverty guideline for the borrower's family size and state. For 2026 the federal poverty guideline is $15,650 for a family of one, with an additional $5,580 for each additional family member; 150 percent of the family-of-one figure is $23,475, and 150 percent of the family-of-two figure is $31,845. A single borrower with a 2025 adjusted gross income of $40,000 has discretionary income of roughly $16,525, which produces a 15 percent rehabilitation payment of about $207 per month. A married couple filing jointly with a combined AGI of $55,000 and no additional dependents has discretionary income of roughly $23,155 and a 15 percent payment of about $289 per month.

The reasonable-and-affordable alternative replaces that formula entirely for borrowers who can document that the statutory figure does not reflect their actual ability to pay. The alternative has no fixed formula. The borrower submits a documented monthly expense statement listing rent or mortgage, utilities, food, transportation, medical expenses, child care, and other recurring costs, and the servicer must reduce the rehabilitation payment to an amount the borrower can actually afford given income minus expenses. The absolute floor under 34 CFR 685.211(f) is $5 per month. Most borrowers who submit a documented expense statement end up with a rehabilitation payment between $5 and $50 per month, regardless of the 15 percent statutory calculation. This is the single most important thing defaulted borrowers do not know: servicers will quote the 15 percent figure first and the reasonable-and-affordable floor only if the borrower explicitly asks.

The practical move is to open the rehabilitation conversation with a specific written request for a reasonable-and-affordable determination, not a general phone inquiry. Submit it in writing because servicers are required to respond to written reasonable-and-affordable requests in writing with a specific documented payment amount, and the written record serves as proof if the account is later transferred to a different collections contractor. The Written Request Template

Four elements make a reasonable-and-affordable request effective: a specific dollar amount the borrower is requesting, a documented monthly expense schedule, a brief factual narrative on why the statutory 15 percent figure is unaffordable, and a clear statement that this is a written request under 34 CFR 685.211(f) for a reasonable-and-affordable determination. Here is the structure that produces the fastest servicer response:

Open with: "I am writing under 34 CFR 685.211(f) to request a reasonable-and-affordable payment determination for the rehabilitation of my defaulted federal student loans listed below. I am requesting a monthly payment of $[amount] based on the documented income and expense information attached." Follow with the account numbers of each defaulted loan, the borrower's monthly gross income from all sources, and an itemized monthly expense list covering rent or mortgage, utilities (electric, gas, water, phone, internet), food, transportation (car payment, insurance, fuel, public transit), medical expenses (insurance premium, prescriptions, routine care), child care or dependent care, any required work-related expenses, and other recurring costs. End with: "I am requesting that this payment amount be set under the reasonable-and-affordable standard rather than the 15 percent of discretionary income formula, and I am requesting a written confirmation of the agreed payment amount before the first scheduled payment. I intend to establish ACH autopay for this amount upon receipt of the written confirmation."

Submit by the servicer's secure message portal on studentaid.gov or by certified mail to the default resolution team. Servicers typically respond to a well-documented reasonable-and-affordable request within 10 to 20 business days. If the servicer's response quotes a higher payment than the borrower believes the documentation supports, the borrower can submit a written appeal with additional documentation; appeals typically add 15 to 30 days to the start date, so a November 11 appeal that resolves on December 10 still leaves enough calendar to complete the rehabilitation before the August 2027 garnishment-wave window closes.

Rehabilitation vs Consolidation Out of Default

Rehabilitation is one of two routes out of default; the other is consolidation. The two are not interchangeable, and the right choice depends on the borrower's time pressure, credit-report goals, and forgiveness trajectory. Consolidation out of default is faster: the borrower either makes 3 voluntary consecutive monthly payments on the defaulted loan and then consolidates, or signs an agreement to repay the new Direct Consolidation Loan under an income-driven plan (RAP or IBR) with no advance payments required. The entire consolidation process typically completes in 60 to 90 days, compared with 9 to 10 months for rehabilitation.

The tradeoffs are real on both sides. Consolidation does not remove the default record from the borrower's credit report; the default tradeline remains for 7 years from the original delinquency date, even though the defaulted loan itself is paid off by the consolidation. Rehabilitation removes the default record entirely at the three major credit bureaus, which typically produces a 50 to 120 point FICO improvement in the 60 to 120 days following completion. Rehabilitation can only be used once per loan in a borrower's lifetime, so a borrower who used rehabilitation in a prior default cycle is limited to consolidation this time. Consolidation collapses multiple original loans into a single new Direct Consolidation Loan, which can simplify payment management but which also resets the clock on some older-loan benefits and may affect IDR Account Adjustment payment carryover. Compare the two routes directly with the Plan Comparison tool before committing.

The decision framework for most defaulted borrowers in November 2026: choose rehabilitation if (a) this is the first default on the loans, (b) credit-report improvement matters for an upcoming mortgage or auto loan application, and (c) the borrower can afford 9 months of the reasonable-and-affordable payment. Choose consolidation if (a) the loan has already been rehabilitated once in a prior default cycle, (b) the borrower is pursuing PSLF and wants to begin accumulating qualifying months as soon as possible, or (c) wage garnishment or Treasury offset is already active and the borrower needs to stop collection activity in 60 to 90 days rather than wait 9 to 10 months. What Rehabilitation Fixes and What It Does Not

Completed rehabilitation restores the borrower to a good-standing federal student loan status with four specific effects. First, the default tradeline is deleted from all three major credit reports; the individual late payments preceding the default remain on the report for 7 years from the original delinquency date, but the "default" or "charge-off" status itself is gone. Second, collection activity ends: Treasury Offset Program interception of federal tax refunds stops, administrative wage garnishment orders are lifted, and the Department of Justice ends any pending collection referral. Third, the borrower regains eligibility for income-driven repayment plans (RAP, IBR), deferment, forbearance, and additional federal student aid for future enrollment, which rehabilitates the Pell Grant eligibility that was lost in default. Fourth, collection costs that were added to the loan balance (typically 16 to 25 percent of the pre-default balance) remain on the new rehabilitated loan but stop accruing further; any capitalized interest from the default period remains on the balance as well.

Rehabilitation does not do several things. It does not erase the 9 months of individual late payments that preceded the default; those stay on the report for 7 years. It does not count the 9 rehabilitation payments as qualifying PSLF, RAP, or IDR forgiveness payments. It does not refund past tax-refund interceptions or past wage garnishment amounts. It does not undo capitalized interest or waive the collection costs added to the balance before rehabilitation began. And it does not prevent a future default; if the borrower falls 270 days behind on the new rehabilitated loan, the loan can default again, but this time without eligibility for a second rehabilitation.

The November 2026 Filing Sequence

A practical filing sequence for a borrower starting the rehabilitation stream the week of November 11, 2026: (1) Log in to studentaid.gov and confirm which loans are in default and which collections contractor holds each loan (the Default Resolution Group handles most of them). (2) Pull your most recent two months of pay stubs and build the monthly expense documentation package. (3) Draft the reasonable-and-affordable written request using the template above with a specific target payment amount. (4) Submit through the servicer's secure portal and request written confirmation of the agreed payment amount. (5) Within 20 business days, receive the servicer's determination and establish ACH autopay for the agreed amount. (6) Make the first payment in November 2026 and continue monthly through July or August 2027.

After the ninth payment posts, the servicer reports rehabilitation completion to the credit bureaus (typically within 30 days of the final payment) and transfers the loan from the default resolution contractor back to a standard loan servicer. At that transfer point, the borrower enrolls in RAP or another income-driven plan at studentaid.gov to begin accumulating PSLF or IDR qualifying months from the first post-rehabilitation payment forward. Three Timing Mistakes That Reset the 9-Month Clock

Mistake 1: Letting wage garnishment proceed while rehabilitation payments are also being made. A payment seized through administrative wage garnishment does not count toward the 9 rehabilitation payments because it is not voluntary. A borrower whose wages are being garnished for $300 and who also sends a $50 voluntary rehabilitation payment is paying $350 per month but earning credit for only one rehabilitation month. The practical fix is to file a request to pause garnishment during rehabilitation, which the Department of Education grants for the duration of an active rehabilitation agreement; submit the request simultaneously with the reasonable-and-affordable request. If the AWG continues despite the pause request, file a formal hearing request within 30 days of the next garnishment paycheck.

Mistake 2: Making the ninth payment early in anticipation of catching up. The 9 payments must each fall in a separate calendar month. A borrower who sends both the September and October payments in September does not finish the 9-payment stream in October; they finish in November with a September double-payment counting as one month. Space the payments one per month and use autopay to eliminate the double-payment scenario.

Mistake 3: Consolidating or taking out a new federal loan during the rehabilitation stream. A new Direct Consolidation Loan that includes any of the loans currently being rehabilitated cancels the rehabilitation progress entirely; the borrower is placed into consolidation instead and loses the credit-report removal benefit. A new federal student loan for current-year enrollment (parent PLUS, grad PLUS, Direct Unsubsidized) also complicates rehabilitation because federal aid awarded during default status may be delayed until rehabilitation completes. If an in-school borrower needs new federal aid during the rehabilitation window, coordinate with the financial aid office and the servicer before accepting any new award.

The Bottom Line

Rehabilitation that starts in the week of November 11, 2026 completes in August 2027, which is the single best timing in the current collections calendar. The second Department of Education administrative wage garnishment wave is projected to land in employer mailrooms starting in September 2027, and a completed rehabilitation shuts that path off before any order can be issued. The reasonable-and-affordable standard with a $5 floor is available by written request to anyone whose documented expenses do not support the 15 percent statutory calculation, which includes most defaulted borrowers. The 9 payments do not count toward PSLF or IDR forgiveness, but completed rehabilitation unlocks RAP enrollment and starts the qualifying-payment clock on the first post-rehabilitation payment, which turns a stuck situation into a funded one.
