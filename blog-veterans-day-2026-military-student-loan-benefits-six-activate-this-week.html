Veterans Day 2026: Six Student Loan Benefits Military Borrowers and Veterans Can Activate This Week

Veterans Day falls on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The week leading up to it is the single best filing window of the year for military student loan benefits: servicers are staffed, the holiday itself is a federal day off that pauses processing deadlines only for one day, and most servicers run a documentation push in the last two weeks of November to clear backlog before the December freeze. Six specific benefits are available to active-duty service members, Guard and Reserve members on qualifying orders, and veterans. Each has a known 2026 filing path, a document checklist, and a processing timeline. This guide walks all six, how they stack with RAP and PSLF, and the three mistakes that delay or deny them.

Military student loan benefits are among the most underused parts of the federal student loan system. In a 2024 Department of Education internal review, fewer than 40 percent of eligible active-duty service members had filed the SCRA 6 percent cap on at least one qualifying loan, and fewer than 20 percent of VA-rated 100 percent disabled veterans had received the automatic TPD discharge the law entitles them to. The gap is not eligibility. It is paperwork, timing, and the fact that each benefit has its own filing path, its own documentation requirements, and its own interaction with RAP, IBR, and PSLF. Veterans Day week is useful because every benefit on this list can be initiated from a single studentaid.gov login plus one written request to the servicer, and most can be filed start-to-finish before Thanksgiving.

Three practical notes before the benefits themselves. First, service connection matters more than discharge status for most of these benefits; a veteran with an honorable discharge who never served in a declared hostile area has access to a different set of benefits than a current active-duty service member in a designated combat zone. Second, the SCRA 6 percent cap and the 0 percent hostile-area interest benefit apply retroactively if filed late, which makes "this week" a convenience, not a deadline. Third, every benefit on this list stacks with RAP's interest waiver and 50 percent principal match, so the practical goal is to layer them: lower the rate first, then enroll in the right repayment plan, then file for PSLF credit on months that qualify. Benefit 1: The SCRA 6 Percent Interest Rate Cap

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act caps the interest rate on any student loan (federal or private) taken out BEFORE the borrower began active-duty service at 6 percent for the entire period of active duty. Interest above 6 percent is forgiven rather than deferred, which means the borrower does not owe it when active duty ends. The cap applies to full-time active-duty service in the regular Armed Forces and to National Guard and Reserve members called to active federal service for a consecutive period of 30 days or more under Title 10 or Title 32 orders.

The 2026 filing path is a written request to each servicer holding a qualifying loan. Include a copy of the active-duty orders, the dates of service, the full list of loan account numbers you want capped, and an explicit request that the cap apply retroactively to the first day of qualifying service if that is earlier than the filing date. Most servicers process the request in 30 to 45 days and apply the rate reduction to the next billing cycle. If the loan is on autopay with a 0.25 percent discount, confirm in the request that the autopay discount remains in effect on top of the SCRA cap (it should). Interest that accrued above 6 percent in the retroactive period is removed from the balance, not refunded as a check.

Benefit 2: 0 Percent Interest for Service in a Hostile Area

Section 455(o) of the Higher Education Act goes further than the SCRA cap for Direct Loan borrowers serving in a hostile area. Any Direct Subsidized, Direct Unsubsidized, or Direct Consolidation Loan accrues zero percent interest for up to 60 months of qualifying service in an area where the service member receives imminent-danger or hostile-fire pay under 37 U.S.C. 310. Unlike the SCRA cap, which applies only to pre-service debt, the HEA 455(o) 0 percent rate applies to all qualifying Direct Loans regardless of origination date. It also does not require a 30-day minimum of service; the 0 percent rate applies month by month for any month in which the service member received hostile-pay duty days.

The filing path is the same written request to the servicer, but the required documentation adds a Statement of Service listing hostile-pay duty days. Request one from the service member's personnel office; it takes about 10 business days to receive. The SCRA 6 percent cap and the HEA 455(o) 0 percent rate stack: pre-service loans at 6 percent under SCRA, Direct Loans at 0 percent under HEA 455(o). For a borrower with a $40,000 Direct Loan balance deploying for 12 months to a hostile-pay area, the 0 percent rate saves roughly $2,800 in interest at 2026 rates compared with the 6 percent SCRA cap and roughly $3,400 compared with the uncapped 8 percent rate on 2026-27 origination.

Benefit 3: PSLF for Active-Duty Service and VA Civilian Employment

Each branch of the United States Armed Forces is a qualifying public-service employer for Public Service Loan Forgiveness, and so is every civilian position within the Department of Veterans Affairs. A service member who enrolls in RAP or IBR during active duty and makes 120 qualifying monthly payments earns full forgiveness of the remaining balance. Because active-duty pay at lower ranks produces a low AGI, the qualifying monthly payment under RAP or IBR during active duty is often $0 or $10, which means the 120-payment PSLF clock is accumulating at near-zero out-of-pocket cost. For E-1 through E-4 service members with a single AGI and no dependents, RAP's 2026 $10 minimum payment typically governs; model the actual number with the RAP Calculator.

The annual filing sequence for military PSLF is straightforward: enroll in RAP or IBR at studentaid.gov, submit a PSLF Employer Certification Form (ECF) with the service member's commanding officer or personnel office as the certifier, and track the count in the PSLF Help Tool. Veterans who transition to VA civilian employment follow the same process with VA human resources as the certifier. Guard and Reserve members earn PSLF credit only during months when they are on Title 10 federal active orders of 30 days or more; pure drill-weekend service does not qualify. The PSLF Tracker models the full 120-month clock based on your current count and projected service timeline.

Benefit 4: Military Service Deferment

The Military Service Deferment postpones federal student loan payments during a period of active-duty service connected to a war, military operation, or national emergency, and it extends for 13 months after active duty ends or until the borrower returns to enrolled status at an eligible school. Interest on Direct Subsidized Loans, subsidized FFEL Stafford Loans, and the subsidized portion of Direct Consolidation Loans does not accrue during deferment. Interest on unsubsidized loans continues to accrue but does not capitalize when the deferment ends unless the borrower exits the deferment and enters repayment under a plan that would otherwise capitalize.

The filing path is a written deferment request to the servicer with active-duty orders and dates of service attached. The tradeoff to understand before filing: deferred months do not count as qualifying PSLF payments unless the service member stays on active duty throughout and makes voluntary qualifying payments during the deferment. For most PSLF pursuers, the better move is to stay enrolled in RAP or IBR with a $10 minimum payment rather than defer, because every RAP month is a qualifying PSLF month at $10 cost. The deferment is worthwhile when the service member faces a budget crisis that makes even a $10 payment impossible, when the service member has no interest in PSLF and simply wants to pause interest accrual on subsidized loans, or when a credit-reporting concern requires an affirmatively in-good-standing status that deferment provides faster than enrollment. Benefit 5: TPD Discharge via a VA 100 Percent Disability Rating

Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) discharge cancels all federal student loans for veterans with a VA determination of 100 percent service-connected disability that is "totally disabling and permanent in nature," or with a VA determination of Individual Unemployability that is designated as permanent. The 2021 automation agreement between the Department of Education and the VA remained in place through 2026; qualifying veteran data moves from the VA to the Department of Education on a rolling basis, and the Department issues automatic discharges followed by a mailed notification to the veteran's address of record. For most qualifying veterans, no application is required.

If a qualifying veteran has not received a discharge notification within 90 days of a VA 100 percent determination or IU grant, the manual filing path is Form TPD-DISC submitted at disabilitydischarge.com (the Department of Education's TPD portal) with a copy of the VA determination letter attached. Discharge is retroactive to the effective date of the qualifying VA determination, which produces refunds of payments made after that date on most Direct Loans and FFEL loans held by the Department. Payments made to private student loans do not refund under TPD; private lenders handle disability discharge separately and typically less favorably. After the discharge is finalized, the borrower is placed in a three-year monitoring period during which they cannot earn new federal student loans or exceed certain income thresholds without triggering reinstatement, though the three-year monitoring rule was substantially narrowed in 2023 and covers only employment-based income tests at this point.

Benefit 6: HEROES Act Emergency Protections

The Higher Education Relief Opportunities for Students Act of 2003 (HEROES Act) gives the Secretary of Education authority to waive or modify federal student loan requirements for borrowers serving on active duty during a war, military operation, or national emergency, and for certain civilians in affected disaster areas. In 2026 the HEROES Act continues to support three practical use cases for military borrowers. First, administrative forbearance can be applied immediately upon notification of unexpected mobilization, without the usual documentation requirements. Second, in-school enrollment status can be maintained for service members whose deployment interrupts an academic term. Third, the Secretary can waive paperwork requirements that would otherwise require a borrower on active duty to submit forms, recertifications, or employer letters during deployment.

The filing path for a HEROES Act forbearance is a phone call or written request to the servicer citing active-duty mobilization and attaching orders when available. Most servicers maintain a dedicated military team that can process a HEROES Act forbearance within one business day. The forbearance does not count against the nine-month lifetime general forbearance cap and does not disqualify future RAP enrollment. For PSLF pursuers, HEROES Act forbearance months accrued during active duty may count as qualifying under the IDR Account Adjustment carryover rules, which the Department of Education applies automatically on the first PSLF count run after the forbearance ends.

How These Benefits Stack With RAP in 2026

The 2026 RAP plan adds two features that interact with every military benefit on this list: a 100 percent interest waiver on qualifying monthly payments and a 50 percent principal match on every scheduled payment. For a military borrower who stacks the SCRA 6 percent cap, the HEA 455(o) 0 percent hostile-area rate, and RAP's interest waiver, the practical interest cost during a qualifying deployment can be zero, with every dollar of the scheduled payment attacking principal plus the 50 percent match on top. The Payoff Calculator models the combined effect; the typical outcome for a $45,000 balance during a 12-month hostile-area deployment on RAP is a balance reduction of roughly $9,800, compared with roughly $2,200 under standard repayment at full rate.

The stacking order matters for the filing sequence. File the rate caps first (SCRA, then HEA 455(o)), because they reduce interest accrual immediately. File RAP enrollment second, because the monthly payment calculation uses the current interest accrual pattern. File PSLF ECF third, because the ECF counts the RAP enrollment months. Submit the TPD discharge application only if the VA 100 percent determination is in hand; a TPD discharge ends the need for every other benefit on this list except the retroactive interest refund windows, which should be closed first.

Three Mistakes That Delay or Deny Military Benefits

Mistake 1: Submitting active-duty orders without a clear start date and loan list. The single most common servicer rejection in 2026 is a SCRA request that attaches orders but does not name the specific loan accounts the cap should apply to. Servicers process one account per written request unless the request specifies otherwise. Include every federal loan account number, every private loan account number, and an explicit "please apply to all loans listed above" line.

Mistake 2: Filing Military Service Deferment when enrollment in RAP produces a better PSLF outcome. Many service members default to deferment because it feels like the military-specific option, but deferment pauses PSLF credit while a $10 RAP payment accumulates it. For any service member with a reasonable PSLF trajectory, run the PSLF Tracker under both scenarios before filing deferment.

Mistake 3: Assuming the VA TPD automation will arrive without checking. The automation agreement with the Department of Education works for most qualifying veterans but not all; the most common gap is a VA determination that was recently upgraded to 100 percent or a change in last-known mailing address. If a qualifying veteran has not received a TPD notification within 90 days of the VA determination, file Form TPD-DISC manually rather than waiting indefinitely.

The Veterans Day Week Filing Checklist

A practical Veterans Day week workflow for an active-duty service member or veteran who has filed none of these benefits: (1) Pull active-duty orders, dates of service, and a Statement of Service from personnel. (2) Log into studentaid.gov and export a loan summary showing every federal loan account. (3) Send a written SCRA request to each servicer naming every loan to cap. (4) If serving in a hostile-pay area, send a parallel HEA 455(o) request with the Statement of Service attached. (5) Enroll in RAP at studentaid.gov if not already enrolled. (6) Submit a PSLF ECF with command signature. (7) If a VA 100 percent determination is in hand and no automatic discharge arrived, file Form TPD-DISC at disabilitydischarge.com. Steps 1 through 6 take roughly four hours of actual work time distributed across a week; step 7 takes another hour if needed.

The Bottom Line

Veterans Day 2026 is the right occasion to run the full stack. The six benefits on this list are not new and are not scheduled to change in 2027, but every month a qualifying borrower does not file is a month of 6 or 8 percent interest accruing instead of 6 or 0 percent, a month of PSLF credit missed on active duty, or a month of post-determination TPD payment sitting un-refunded. Interest caps are retroactive within the servicer's documentation window, deferments start on the request date, and PSLF credit requires the ECF to be submitted. The forms are free, the Department of Education staff are available this week, and the math is one-way positive.
