1099-C and 1098-E in January 2027: What RAP and Forgiveness Borrowers Need to Gather in November 2026

Student loan servicers mail two tax forms in late January 2027 that look routine but will surprise a lot of borrowers this year. The 1098-E (Student Loan Interest Statement) will come in materially smaller for RAP borrowers because the plan's 100 percent interest waiver zeroes out interest before it is paid. The 1099-C (Cancellation of Debt) will arrive for every borrower whose loans were forgiven in 2026 under IDR, TPD, closed-school, borrower-defense, PSLF, or Teacher Loan Forgiveness, and tax year 2026 is the first year since 2020 that IDR-based forgiveness is federally taxable. November is the window to update addresses, opt into electronic delivery, export payment histories, and pull the insolvency snapshot that a Form 982 filing would require. This guide walks both forms and the full prep checklist.

Two things changed between the 2025 tax year and the 2026 tax year that make this January different from the past five. First, the American Rescue Plan Act's temporary federal exclusion of student loan forgiveness from taxable income expired on December 31, 2025. Forgiveness that happens in tax year 2026 is federally taxable unless a narrower, permanent federal exclusion (PSLF, TPD, closed-school, borrower-defense, Teacher Loan Forgiveness) applies. Second, the 2026 RAP plan introduced a 100 percent interest waiver that reduces the 1098-E figure for borrowers who stayed current, and a 50 percent principal match that is reported separately on the servicer's year-end statement rather than on the 1098-E itself. Both changes produce new line items that tax software is only starting to handle well.

The November prep window matters because mailed 1099-C forms can arrive up to three weeks after the January 31 statutory deadline, address-of-record updates with the Department of Education take 10 to 14 business days to propagate across servicers, and electronic-delivery consent requires an affirmative opt-in that cannot be retroactively applied once forms have been generated. A borrower who opts into electronic delivery by November 30 will typically see both forms in the servicer portal on or around January 20, 2027. A borrower who does not will often not see the paper form until mid-February, after their tax software has already asked for the number. Before any filing work, model the practical effect of forgiveness on a specific loan balance with the Payoff Calculator and confirm the remaining balance that would be forgiven under a specific RAP timeline using the RAP Calculator.

What Changed on January 1, 2026: The Expired Federal Exclusion

Section 9675 of the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 added a temporary provision to Internal Revenue Code Section 108(f)(5) that excluded most types of student loan forgiveness from gross income for tax years 2021 through 2025. The exclusion covered IDR forgiveness, administrative discharges, loan cancellation by state programs, and employer-paid student loan assistance. The provision expired on December 31, 2025, and Congress did not extend it. For tax year 2026, the pre-2021 rules apply: student loan forgiveness is federal ordinary income unless a narrower, permanent federal exclusion applies.

The permanent exclusions still in force for 2026 are specific and limited. PSLF forgiveness under IRC Section 108(f)(1) is permanently excluded because PSLF requires qualifying public-service employment. TPD discharge is permanently excluded under the Internal Revenue Code's disability-specific provisions. Closed-school and borrower-defense discharges are permanently excluded because they correct a prior improper lending. Teacher Loan Forgiveness is permanently excluded. The Section 127 employer-provided educational assistance exclusion of up to $5,250 per year remains in effect and covers employer-paid student loan assistance that was added to Section 127 in 2020. Every other type of forgiveness, including IDR forgiveness at the end of 20 or 25 years and discharge under private loan settlements, defaults into taxable ordinary income for 2026.

The 1098-E: Why RAP Borrowers Will See a Smaller Number

Form 1098-E reports the student loan interest a borrower actually paid during the tax year on qualifying federal and private student loans. The amount reported in Box 1 flows to the above-the-line student loan interest deduction of up to $2,500 per return on Schedule 1, Line 21 of Form 1040. The deduction phases out between $85,000 and $100,000 of modified AGI for single filers and between $175,000 and $205,000 of modified AGI for joint filers in 2026. Only interest you actually paid counts; interest that was waived, forgiven, or capitalized but not paid does not.

The 2026 RAP plan's 100 percent interest waiver changes the math significantly. Under RAP, for each month a borrower makes the scheduled payment on time, the servicer waives any interest that would otherwise accrue beyond the amount the scheduled payment covers. The waiver happens before the borrower pays, which means the borrower never pays the waived interest and cannot deduct it. For a typical RAP borrower with a $45,000 balance and a $120 scheduled monthly payment, the interest actually paid in 2026 might be $800 for the year, compared with $3,200 of interest that would have been paid under the old SAVE plan at the same balance and income. The 1098-E reports the $800, the student loan interest deduction applies to the $800, and the borrower's deduction is $800 rather than the capped $2,500.

This is not a disadvantage overall. The lost deduction is at most $2,500 x marginal rate, which for a 22 percent marginal borrower is $550 of foregone tax savings. The RAP interest waiver and 50 percent principal match reduce lifetime loan cost by five to ten times that amount for most borrowers. But borrowers who were accustomed to taking the full $2,500 deduction each year and who planned withholding around it should adjust 2026 Q4 estimated taxes or W-4 withholding to reflect the smaller deduction. The 1099-C: What It Reports and Which Exclusion Applies

Form 1099-C (Cancellation of Debt) is the IRS information return that reports debt cancellation of $600 or more. Student loan servicers file 1099-C forms with the IRS and mail copies to borrowers by January 31 for forgiveness that occurred in the prior tax year. The form has four boxes borrowers read most often. Box 1 is the date of cancellation (the effective discharge date on the servicer's letter, not the application date). Box 2 is the amount of debt cancelled. Box 3 is the interest included in Box 2. Box 6 is the identifiable event code: Code A (Bankruptcy), Code F (Agreement to discontinue collection), Code G (Decision or policy to discontinue collection), and Code H (Expiration of nonpayment testing period) are the codes most relevant to student loan forgiveness.

The amount in Box 2 flows to Schedule 1, Line 8c (Cancellation of debt) of Form 1040 unless an exclusion applies. If the forgiveness qualifies for a federal exclusion (PSLF, TPD, closed-school, borrower-defense, Teacher Loan Forgiveness, insolvency, bankruptcy), the borrower files Form 982 (Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to Discharge of Indebtedness) to claim the exclusion. Form 982 has a checkbox for each specific exclusion code; the borrower checks the appropriate box in Part I and enters the excluded amount on Line 2. The IRS compares the 1099-C Box 2 amount against the Form 982 excluded amount; a discrepancy triggers an automated notice.

For PSLF borrowers specifically, the servicer-issued 1099-C in 2026 typically carries Code F and is accompanied by a letter from the Department of Education stating that the discharge qualifies under IRC Section 108(f)(1). Attach a copy of the letter to the 2026 return filed in 2027 along with Form 982 showing the full Box 2 amount excluded under PSLF. Save the PSLF Tracker output as supporting documentation; a saved PDF of the 120-payment count timeline substantiates the discharge if the IRS asks for proof of the qualifying event.

The Insolvency Exclusion on Form 982 for IDR Borrowers

IDR forgiveness in 2026 does not qualify for a federal exclusion simply because it is IDR forgiveness. The expired American Rescue Plan provision was the IDR-specific exclusion, and Congress did not replace it. For an IDR borrower who reached the 20-year or 25-year forgiveness mark in 2026, the full Box 2 amount flows to Schedule 1 unless the borrower was insolvent immediately before the cancellation.

The insolvency exclusion under IRC Section 108(a)(1)(B) applies when total liabilities exceed the fair market value of total assets immediately before the cancellation event. The exclusion is limited to the amount of the insolvency. A borrower with $90,000 of forgiven debt, $150,000 of liabilities, and $80,000 of assets immediately before cancellation is insolvent by $70,000 and excludes $70,000; the remaining $20,000 is taxable. A borrower with $90,000 of forgiven debt, $200,000 of liabilities, and $50,000 of assets is insolvent by $150,000 and excludes the full $90,000 of forgiven debt.

The insolvency measurement is as of the moment immediately before the cancellation, which means the servicer's effective discharge date is the governing moment. For a discharge dated October 15, 2026, the insolvency calculation uses the borrower's balance sheet on October 14, 2026. Documentation to gather in November 2026 if a 2026 discharge is expected or has already occurred: dated balance statements for every retirement account (401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, 403(b), HSA when applicable), every bank account, every investment account, the fair market value of real estate (a Zillow or Redfin estimate is acceptable for the initial calculation; a formal appraisal is stronger if the IRS requests documentation), the fair market value of vehicles (Kelley Blue Book), and the balance of every debt (including the student loan debt being forgiven, which counts as a liability immediately before the cancellation). Retirement accounts count as assets for the insolvency calculation.

A practical November 2026 move: take a dated PDF screenshot of every account balance and every debt statement this month, save a copy of your Zillow and Kelley Blue Book estimates, and save a copy of each student loan balance from studentaid.gov. If the 1099-C arrives in late January with a 2026 effective date, the November snapshot plus an updated snapshot as of the actual discharge date together produce a defensible insolvency calculation on Form 982.

State Conformity: The 15 States Still Exempting Forgiveness in 2026

State income taxes follow state law, not federal law, and states vary in whether they tax student loan forgiveness. Many states "conform" to the Internal Revenue Code either automatically (rolling conformity) or by statute at a fixed date (static conformity). Rolling-conformity states that excluded forgiveness through 2025 because the federal exclusion existed now tax it in 2026 unless the state legislature enacted an independent exclusion. Static-conformity states that incorporated the federal code as of a date before January 1, 2026, may continue to exclude forgiveness depending on the conformity date.

States that still exempt student loan forgiveness from state income tax for tax year 2026, based on legislation in effect in November 2026, include California (through 2029 for borrowers meeting state income limits), New York, Pennsylvania (does not tax most types), Virginia (rolling conformity statute carries forward), Massachusetts, Illinois, New Jersey, Hawaii, North Dakota, Delaware, Vermont, Rhode Island, Maine, Connecticut (most types), and Oregon. States that fully tax IDR forgiveness in 2026 include Indiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, Arkansas, and Wisconsin. Most other states have intermediate rules or are awaiting legislative clarification; always verify with your state's Department of Revenue in December 2026 or January 2027, when state tax conformity for the full year is confirmed.

The November 2026 Prep Checklist

A practical window to run before Thanksgiving:

1. Verify your mailing address of record. Log into studentaid.gov and confirm the address on file matches where you want mailed tax forms to arrive. Then log into each servicer's portal (MOHELA, Nelnet, Aidvantage, EdFinancial, ECSI as applicable) and confirm the same address. Address changes take 10 to 14 business days to propagate.

2. Opt into electronic delivery of tax documents. Each servicer has a separate electronic consent form; sign each one. Electronic forms post to the portal on or around January 20, two to three weeks before paper forms arrive.

3. Export a full 2026 payment history from each servicer. The payment history is the primary source document for interest paid, payments made, and discharge effective dates. Download a PDF through November 30, 2026 (year-end data posts after January 1).

4. Save a current loan balance snapshot. A dated PDF of your current balance from studentaid.gov supports Form 982 insolvency calculations and reconciles against the 1099-C Box 2 amount when it arrives.

5. If forgiveness is expected or has occurred in 2026, request a servicer discharge letter. Ask each servicer that holds forgiven debt for a letter confirming the effective discharge date, the amount forgiven, and the applicable identifiable event code (A, F, G, or H). The letter shortens the audit trail if the 1099-C arrives with an incorrect code.

6. Pull your 2025 1098-E for comparison. RAP borrowers will see a materially smaller 2026 figure. Having the 2025 form in hand lets you confirm the 2026 figure makes sense and lets you adjust W-4 withholding or Q4 estimated tax to reflect the smaller deduction.

7. For PSLF, TPD, closed-school, or borrower-defense borrowers, save every exclusion letter. Form 982 requires documentation of the exclusion. Save PDFs of the discharge letter, the PSLF Tracker output showing the 120-payment count (for PSLF), the VA 100 percent determination letter (for TPD), and any Department of Education communication confirming the exclusion applies.

8. For IDR forgiveness recipients, take a dated insolvency snapshot. PDF screenshots of every account balance and every debt statement as of the current date, plus Zillow/Redfin and Kelley Blue Book estimates for real estate and vehicles. Save in a folder titled with the current date.

Three Mistakes That Create Tax-Season Scrambles

Mistake 1: Waiting for the paper 1099-C before starting tax preparation. Mailed 1099-C forms routinely arrive in mid-February. Tax software asks for the number in early January for refund-chasers and in March for average filers. Opt into electronic delivery now.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the state tax question. Even in states that exempt federal forgiveness, the state return often requires a specific addition modification on the state's Schedule to back out the federal inclusion; filing the federal return and forgetting the state modification produces an overpayment the state will not refund automatically. Review your state Department of Revenue 2026 tax guidance in January 2027.

Mistake 3: Filing Form 982 insolvency without the dated balance sheet. The IRS routinely asks for insolvency support when Form 982 excludes a large amount. A dated balance sheet taken within 30 days of the cancellation event, with account statements and property valuations attached, is the standard documentation. A balance sheet reconstructed after the fact from memory is not.

The Bottom Line

The 1098-E and 1099-C your servicer will send in January 2027 both look like routine tax documents, but both carry 2026-specific wrinkles. The 1098-E will be smaller than expected for RAP borrowers because the interest waiver zeroes out interest before it is paid. The 1099-C will arrive for every 2026 forgiveness recipient, and for IDR-based forgiveness the amount in Box 2 flows straight to taxable income unless an exclusion applies. The federal exclusions that remain in force (PSLF, TPD, closed-school, borrower-defense, Teacher Loan Forgiveness) are narrower than the expired IDR exclusion, and the insolvency exclusion on Form 982 requires contemporaneous balance-sheet documentation. November is the planning window to update addresses, opt into electronic delivery, pull payment histories, and snapshot the balance sheet. Model the full picture before filing: use the RAP Calculator to estimate 2026 interest paid, the Payoff Calculator to project 2026 principal reduction, the Plan Comparison tool to compare deduction outcomes across plans, and the PSLF Tracker to confirm qualifying payment counts if discharge is pending. Four weeks of November prep is the difference between a January 2027 that is a 20-minute confirmation and one that is a two-month document hunt.
