Black Friday to New Year's Eve 2026: No Tax on Tips ($25,000 Cap), No Tax on Overtime ($12,500 Cap), the Last-Month W-2 Documentation Checklist, and How Service and Hourly Workers Cut Their 2027 RAP Payment

Five weeks remain in the first tax year that the One Big Beautiful Bill's two signature worker-class deductions apply: a $25,000 above-the-line deduction for qualified tip income and a $12,500 above-the-line deduction for the FLSA half-premium portion of qualified overtime pay (double for married filing jointly). For a federal student loan borrower on the Repayment Assistance Plan who is working holiday hospitality, retail, delivery, or extended-hour shifts between Thanksgiving weekend and New Year's Eve, every qualified tip dollar and every qualified overtime-premium dollar that lands on Schedule 1 of the 2026 return reduces AGI directly and lowers the 2027 RAP payment at next year's recertification. This guide walks the IRS April 2026 final qualifying-occupation list, the FLSA half-premium arithmetic, the W-2 Box 14 reporting requirement most employers are navigating for the first time, the contemporaneous tip-log documentation the IRS now expects, the $150,000 / $300,000 phase-out, and the week-by-week November-through-April schedule that turns five weeks of holiday shifts into a measurable 2027 RAP payment reduction.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted in July 2025, created two above-the-line deductions that reduce adjusted gross income for workers in traditionally-tipped service occupations and for hourly workers who receive FLSA-mandated overtime. Section 70201 created new IRC Section 224 authorizing the No Tax on Tips deduction of up to $25,000 per filer; Section 70202 created new IRC Section 225 authorizing the No Tax on Overtime deduction of up to $12,500 per individual filer or $25,000 married filing jointly. Both deductions are effective for tax years 2025 through 2028 and both land on Schedule 1 above the AGI line. The IRS published preliminary guidance in late 2025, interim final regulations in January 2026, and final regulations in April 2026 (Treasury Decision 10046, Federal Register vol. 91 no. 70) defining qualifying occupations and the documentation requirements that make the deductions claimable. Most employers are implementing the new W-2 Box 14 reporting codes for the first time this tax year; the 2026 W-2s mailed in January 2027 are the first W-2s in American history to carry separate line items for qualified tip income and qualified overtime compensation.

For a federal student loan borrower on the Repayment Assistance Plan, these two deductions matter more than the typical worker-class tax benefit because they feed the AGI line that drives the 2027 RAP payment calculation. The 2027 RAP recertification uses the 2026 AGI minus a family-size protected income allowance, times a graduated percentage from one percent on the lowest countable-income tier up to ten percent on the top tier. Anything that reduces 2026 AGI by one dollar reduces RAP-countable income by one dollar, which reduces the 2027 monthly payment by $0.01 to $0.10, or $0.12 to $1.20 across the twelve-month 2027 payment cycle. A full-year $25,000 tip deduction cap, if reached, produces $3,000 to $30,000 of 2027 annual RAP-payment savings at the top of the graduated scale, concentrated in the middle tiers for the typical service-industry RAP borrower. The holiday season between Thanksgiving weekend and December 31 is the single largest tipping and overtime window of the year for most service and hourly workers, which makes the next five weeks the most leveraged documentation period of the entire tax year.

The 2026 AGI to 2027 RAP Payment Translation

The Repayment Assistance Plan payment formula, codified in the RISE final regulations published in May 2026, calculates the monthly payment as follows: start with adjusted gross income from Form 1040 line 11, subtract a protected income allowance equal to 150 percent of the federal poverty line for the household size (for 2026 the FPL is $15,650 plus $5,580 per additional person, so the protected allowance is $23,475 for a one-person household and $38,445 for a two-person household), subtract $50 per month per dependent, then apply a graduated percentage ranging from one percent on the first tier up to ten percent on the top tier. The 2027 recertification uses the most recently filed federal tax return, which for borrowers recertifying between April 2027 and the following March is the 2026 return.

For a single RAP borrower with $55,000 in gross wages (including tip income and overtime) and no other deductions, the pre-OBBBA 2026 AGI is approximately $55,000, which generates a RAP-countable income of about $31,525 (after the $23,475 protected allowance). The graduated-scale payment on $31,525 of countable income is approximately $118 per month, or $1,416 per year. If the same borrower documents $15,000 of qualified tip income and $4,000 of qualified overtime half-premium during the year, both amounts move to Schedule 1 and reduce AGI by $19,000 to $36,000. The RAP-countable income drops to $12,525, and the graduated-scale payment drops to approximately $21 per month, or $252 per year. The combined OBBBA deductions save $1,164 of 2027 RAP payment obligations for this one borrower, continuing across the 2027 payment cycle. No Tax on Tips: The $25,000 Above-the-Line Deduction

New IRC Section 224, enacted in OBBBA Section 70201, authorizes an above-the-line deduction for qualified tip income up to $25,000 per filer per tax year. The deduction is available to any filer regardless of filing status (single, MFJ, MFS, HoH) and does not require itemization; it is claimed on Schedule 1 of Form 1040 whether the taxpayer takes the standard deduction or itemizes. The deduction is not a tax credit; it reduces taxable income rather than directly reducing tax liability, and the tax savings per dollar deducted depends on the taxpayer's marginal bracket. For the RAP borrower the deduction matters primarily because it reduces AGI, which feeds the RAP payment formula.

The qualifying-occupation requirement is codified in the April 2026 final regulations (26 CFR 1.224-1) using the Bureau of Labor Statistics Standard Occupational Classification system. The final list of SOC codes eligible for the tip deduction includes: food and beverage servers (SOC 35-3031), bartenders (SOC 35-3011), cafeteria attendants and counter workers (SOC 35-9031), hair stylists and barbers (SOC 39-5012 and 39-5011), nail technicians and manicurists (SOC 39-5092), estheticians and skincare specialists (SOC 39-5094), massage therapists (SOC 31-9011), taxi and chauffeur drivers (SOC 53-3054), passenger vehicle drivers, delivery drivers (SOC 53-3031 and 53-3032), gaming dealers (SOC 39-3011), ushers and ticket takers (SOC 39-3031), hotel bellhops and porters (SOC 53-7061), concierge staff (SOC 39-6012), hotel desk clerks (SOC 43-4081) where tips are customary, parking attendants (SOC 53-6021), shoe shiners and car-wash workers in traditionally-tipped settings, and a handful of smaller occupational categories. Workers in Specified Service Trades or Businesses under IRC Section 199A (lawyers, doctors, accountants, consultants, financial advisors, performers, athletes) are specifically excluded from the deduction even if they receive tips in the course of their work.

The deduction requires the worker's qualifying occupation to be the primary occupation for the tax year. A software engineer who also works weekend shifts bartending qualifies for the tip deduction on the bartending tips because bartending is a qualifying occupation; the tip income must be tracked and reported separately from the engineering wages, and only the tips earned in the bartending role count. A borrower who moves between a qualifying occupation and a non-qualifying occupation during the year prorates the deduction by the portion of total tips earned in the qualifying role.

Qualified Tips Versus Non-Qualifying Service Charges

A critical distinction in the final regulations is the difference between a qualified tip and a non-qualifying service charge. A qualified tip is paid voluntarily by the customer, in an amount determined by the customer, with no employer compulsion or pre-set schedule. Mandatory service charges, auto-gratuities for large parties, resort fees that include built-in tips, catering service fees, and hotel bellhop fees set by the establishment are treated as wages under existing IRS rules and are reported in box 1 of Form W-2. These do not qualify for the Section 224 deduction regardless of whether the restaurant or hotel distributes the charge to the server.

Qualified tips can be paid in cash, via credit card, through a tipping-app platform (such as Venmo or Zelle direct to the worker in certain states), or pooled and distributed through a tip-share arrangement among tipped employees. Credit card tips reported through the employer's point-of-sale terminal typically flow correctly to Form W-2 Box 14 with the qualified-tip code. Cash tips require the employee to report them to the employer through Form 4070 (Employee Report of Tips to Employer) or the equivalent employer-provided monthly tip report by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the tips were received. Cash tips that go unreported to the employer do not retroactively qualify for the deduction; the deduction is only available for tips documented through the employer's reporting system or, for 1099 workers, through the platform's annual tip summary.

For the Black Friday to New Year's Eve window, the practical consequence is that the single largest action a service worker can take right now to maximize the 2026 tip deduction is to track and report every cash tip received during the heavy-tipping holiday weeks. A contemporaneous daily tip log on Form 4070A (Employee's Daily Record of Tips), filled out at the end of each shift, satisfies the IRS recordkeeping requirement and supports the number reported on Schedule 1 when the 2026 return is filed in early 2027. A paper log works; a note-taking app on a phone also works, as long as the entries are made contemporaneously (not reconstructed from memory months later). The IRS examination risk for tip-deduction claims increases materially when the taxpayer cannot produce a contemporaneous log.

No Tax on Overtime: The $12,500 Above-the-Line Deduction and the FLSA Half-Premium Carve-Out

New IRC Section 225, enacted in OBBBA Section 70202, authorizes an above-the-line deduction for qualified overtime compensation up to $12,500 per individual filer or $25,000 married filing jointly. The deduction is claimed on Schedule 1 above the AGI line and is available regardless of standard-versus-itemized election. The critical structural feature is that the deductible amount is not the full overtime pay but only the FLSA half-premium portion.

A worker with a regular rate of $20 per hour who works 10 FLSA overtime hours at the time-and-a-half rate of $30 per hour earns $300 of overtime gross pay. The regular-rate portion ($20 times 10 hours equals $200) is ordinary wages and is reported in box 1 of Form W-2 as regular compensation. The half-premium portion (the extra $10 per hour times 10 hours equals $100) is the qualified overtime deduction. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must be paid 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek; the Section 225 deduction captures only the "half" above the regular rate, not the full overtime wage. The IRS August 2026 updated guidance (FS-2026-13) walked through the arithmetic in detail; the Treasury intention was to equalize the tax treatment of overtime pay to regular pay for the premium portion, not to exempt all overtime wages from tax.

The deduction is limited to FLSA-mandated overtime only. The federal FLSA requires 1.5x pay for hours over 40 per workweek for non-exempt employees. State-law overtime that goes beyond FLSA (California's daily eight-hour overtime rule under Industrial Welfare Commission Order 16-2001, Alaska's and Nevada's daily eight-hour rules, Colorado's twelve-hour daily rule) produces additional overtime pay under state law but does not qualify for the federal Section 225 deduction because it is not FLSA-mandated. For a California worker who works a 10-hour day with no other days worked that week (eight hours regular plus two hours daily overtime under state law), the two hours of state-law daily overtime do not qualify federally; the Section 225 deduction only applies when the worker also exceeds 40 hours in the workweek.

To reach the full $12,500 annual deduction cap, a worker at a $20 per hour regular rate would need to log 1,250 FLSA overtime hours in a year, which is roughly 24 overtime hours per week for 52 weeks (the worker's total week would be 64 hours). Few workers sustain that pace; the more realistic scenario is a borrower who logs heavy overtime in the November-December holiday retail, logistics, warehouse, hospitality, or delivery season and earns $3,000 to $8,000 of qualified half-premium during the five-week window. That half-premium reduces 2026 AGI directly and reduces the 2027 RAP payment by approximately $300 to $800 across the following year.

Employer Reporting on W-2 Box 14 and the January 2027 Corrected-W-2 Window

The 2026 Form W-2 includes new Box 14 reporting requirements for qualified tips and qualified overtime. The IRS added two specific codes to the Box 14 instructions: TIP for qualified tip income under IRC Section 224, and OT for qualified FLSA half-premium overtime compensation under IRC Section 225. Employers are required to track qualified amounts separately throughout the payroll year and report the totals in Box 14 of the January 2027 W-2. Most payroll software vendors (ADP, Paychex, Gusto, Rippling, Workday, QuickBooks Payroll) released 2026 updates in early 2026 to implement the tracking, but the first year of a new reporting rule produces widespread reporting errors; the IRS acknowledged in its November 2026 payroll-practitioner alert that corrected-W-2 (Form W-2c) filings are expected to run higher than usual for the 2026 tax year.

For a RAP borrower, the practical consequence is that the January 2027 W-2 is the single most important document to verify this tax season. Three checks run quickly: first, confirm that Box 14 contains a line labeled TIP with a dollar figure that matches the borrower's contemporaneous tip log for 2026. Second, confirm that Box 14 contains a line labeled OT with a dollar figure that matches the borrower's expected FLSA half-premium (calculate as total overtime gross pay minus regular-rate pay for overtime hours). Third, confirm that Box 1 wages do not double-count the tip and overtime amounts (the Box 14 figures are informational and must also be included in Box 1 for income tax purposes, but the Schedule 1 deduction then removes them above the AGI line).

If Box 14 is missing the TIP or OT entries, request a corrected W-2c from the employer in writing. Employers generally issue W-2c forms within 30 days of a documented request; the IRS January 31, 2027 W-2 filing deadline sets the original filing date, but corrections can be issued and accepted any time before the taxpayer's 2026 return is filed. Filing the 2026 return without a W-2c and then claiming the Schedule 1 deduction without supporting Box 14 documentation is permitted but increases the IRS examination risk, because the IRS cross-matches W-2 Box 14 to Schedule 1 deductions through automated under-reporter programs.

1099 Workers: Platform Tip Summaries From Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, and Instacart

For independent contractors in qualifying tipped occupations, particularly rideshare drivers and delivery drivers, tip documentation comes through the platform's annual 1099 package rather than a W-2. Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, Grubhub, and Uber Eats each added a separate tip-income summary report for the 2026 tax year, delivered alongside the standard 1099-NEC or 1099-K. The tip summary reports the total tips paid to the driver through the platform during 2026, which is the number the driver uses on Schedule 1 for the Section 224 deduction.

Three documentation issues arise commonly for platform drivers. First, tips paid through the platform are easy to document; tips paid in cash directly by the passenger to the driver are not tracked by the platform and require the driver's own contemporaneous log. A driver who accepts a mix of platform tips and cash tips should maintain a separate cash-tip log on a daily basis. Second, the "tip" versus "bonus" versus "incentive" distinction matters: platform-paid bonuses (Uber's quest payments, DoorDash's Peak Pay guarantee, Lyft's ride-challenge bonuses) are not tips for Section 224 purposes because they come from the platform rather than the passenger. Only the passenger-funded tip component qualifies. Third, drivers who work across multiple platforms must aggregate the tip income from all platforms to compute the total, which can exceed $25,000 in high-tipping markets (New York, San Francisco, Boston, Chicago) during peak seasons.

For drivers on RAP who use a vehicle expense deduction (Schedule C mileage or actual-expense method), the sequence is: compute Schedule C net profit from gross rideshare and delivery income (including tips) minus mileage or vehicle expenses, subtract the Section 224 qualified tip deduction on Schedule 1 (not on Schedule C, which uses the gross figure), and arrive at AGI. The tip deduction is above-the-line and separate from the business expense deduction; both reduce AGI and both compound for RAP payment reduction purposes.

The $150,000 / $300,000 MAGI Phase-Out

Both Section 224 (tips) and Section 225 (overtime) use the same modified adjusted gross income phase-out thresholds: $150,000 single / head of household / MFS, and $300,000 married filing jointly. The deduction amount is reduced by $100 for every $1,000 of MAGI above the applicable threshold, which produces a straight-line phase-out that eliminates the entire tip deduction at $400,000 MAGI single ($25,000 reduced by $250 times $100 equals zero) and the overtime deduction at $275,000 MAGI single. MFJ thresholds eliminate the tip deduction at $550,000 MAGI and the overtime deduction at $425,000 MAGI.

For the typical tipped-service RAP borrower, the phase-out rarely binds; most food-service, personal-care, and hospitality workers earn well below $150,000. The phase-out can bind in two specific scenarios that occur among RAP borrowers more commonly than general tipped workers. First, a married couple where one spouse is a high-earning professional (physician, attorney, finance) and the other is a tipped service worker files jointly; the combined MAGI can push over $300,000 and phase out the tip-earning spouse's deduction. In that scenario, running the MFS calculation is worth doing, because the tip-earning spouse filing separately keeps the $150,000 threshold and can preserve the full deduction. The RAP payment implications of MFS versus MFJ also matter, and the Plan Comparison tool can model both filing-status combinations side by side. Second, a borrower with substantial 1099 side-gig income in a qualifying tipped occupation (nights and weekends driving for Uber in addition to a day job) can exceed $150,000 MAGI when the day-job income is high.

The Black-Friday-to-April Timeline Checklist

Thanksgiving weekend through December 6, 2026: If you work in a qualifying tipped occupation, start a contemporaneous daily tip log today if you have not already. A paper notebook, a notes app, or Form 4070A each work. Record cash tips at the end of each shift. For credit card and platform tips, verify that your employer or platform is tracking them correctly by requesting a mid-year tip summary from the payroll department or downloading the platform's year-to-date earnings report. If you are logging FLSA overtime hours, request your workweek timesheet from payroll to confirm the overtime calculation is correct (regular-rate computation can go wrong when weekly bonuses are included).

Week of December 7 to December 13, 2026: Cash tip workers submit the November Form 4070 (or employer equivalent) to the employer by December 10. Platform drivers download the November earnings statement from each platform and verify the tip breakout. If you have multiple tipped jobs (day-job hair stylist plus weekend bartending), confirm each employer is tracking tips separately on its own payroll system.

Week of December 14 to December 20, 2026: If you are approaching the $25,000 tip deduction cap, consider whether additional December tipping shifts are tax-efficient; income beyond the cap is still taxable at ordinary rates. If you are approaching the $12,500 overtime half-premium cap, the same analysis applies (and the time-and-a-half math means the gross overtime pay at the cap is about $37,500 for a worker with a $20 regular rate, since the cap counts only the half-premium portion). Verify year-to-date W-2 withholding is on track by comparing the December 15 paystub against the approximate 2026 federal tax liability including the new deductions.

Week of December 21 to December 31, 2026: Last five workdays of the tax year. Submit the December Form 4070 to the employer early (by December 31 rather than January 10) so the final W-2 production includes all 2026 tips. Confirm the December paystub shows the correct Box 14 TIP and OT accumulators; some payroll systems do not finalize Box 14 until the January W-2 run, which is fine, but request a year-end payroll summary showing the YTD totals for cross-checking in January.

Week of January 4 to January 31, 2027: W-2s and 1099s arrive. Verify Box 14 TIP and OT figures match your records. If missing or incorrect, request a corrected W-2c from the employer in writing within 30 days. For platform drivers, confirm that each 1099 package includes the tip-income summary; if a platform omits it, request it. Save all documentation in the 2026 tax folder for the Schedule 1 filing.

February 1 to April 15, 2027: File the 2026 return reporting the qualified tip deduction (new Schedule 1 line for Section 224) and qualified overtime deduction (new Schedule 1 line for Section 225). The AGI reduction flows directly to the 2027 RAP recertification. For borrowers on IRS auto-recertification consent, the 2027 RAP payment adjusts automatically within approximately four to eight weeks after the return is processed; for borrowers on manual recertification, upload the 2026 return to the servicer portal during the recertification window. Verify the 2027 payment update with the RAP Calculator once the servicer notice arrives.

The PSLF, Married Filing Separately, and Side-Gig Corner Cases

PSLF interaction. Qualifying monthly RAP payments count toward the 120-payment PSLF requirement under new 34 CFR 685.219 as amended by the 2026 RISE final rule. A lower RAP payment driven by the Section 224 and Section 225 deductions still counts as a qualifying payment for PSLF; the dollar amount of the payment does not affect PSLF payment-count credit. The borrower in a qualifying public-service job who lowers the 2027 RAP payment through tip and overtime deductions therefore gets the double benefit of lower cash outflow and continuing PSLF credit. Track PSLF payment counts with the PSLF Tracker alongside the AGI-reduction planning.

Married filing separately. A borrower running the MFS strategy to shield a higher-earning spouse's income from the RAP calculation can still claim the Section 224 and Section 225 deductions on the MFS return, subject to the $150,000 MAGI phase-out threshold (same as single filer). The MFS filing status reduces the standard deduction to approximately $16,100 for 2026 and eliminates eligibility for several unrelated credits (EITC, Child and Dependent Care Credit), but the tip and overtime deductions remain available. A borrower in a hospitality or service occupation married to a high-earner professional may find that MFS plus the full tip deduction yields a lower combined 2027 RAP payment than MFJ with a phased-out deduction; model both scenarios with the Plan Comparison tool.

Side-gig workers. A primary W-2 employee in a non-qualifying occupation who also drives rideshare or delivery on evenings and weekends can claim the Section 224 deduction on the rideshare/delivery tip income, provided the rideshare role is the worker's qualifying occupation for that income (which it is, since passenger-vehicle and delivery drivers are listed in the final SOC codes). The deduction is computed on the qualifying-income portion only; W-2 wages from the primary job are not deductible even if they include occasional tips (an office receptionist who sometimes gets holiday tips from clients does not qualify, because receptionist is not a listed SOC). Separate recordkeeping by occupation is required, and the Schedule 1 deduction line totals the qualifying tip income across all qualifying roles up to the $25,000 cap.

SAVE-to-RAP transition borrowers. Borrowers who switched from the SAVE plan to RAP during 2026 enter the 2027 recertification cycle on the standard RAP recertification schedule. The 2026 AGI reduction from tip and overtime deductions applies normally to the 2027 payment calculation; the SAVE forbearance months in early 2026 do not change the AGI calculation itself. See the SAVE Transition Guide for the specific recertification timing after a mid-year plan change.

The Bottom Line

The 2025-2028 OBBBA deductions for qualified tip income (up to $25,000 above-the-line under new IRC Section 224) and qualified FLSA overtime half-premium (up to $12,500 single or $25,000 MFJ under new IRC Section 225) are the two largest new AGI-reduction channels for service and hourly workers in American tax history. For a RAP borrower working holiday hospitality, retail, delivery, or extended-hour shifts between Thanksgiving weekend and New Year's Eve 2026, the five-week window is the single most leveraged documentation period of the entire tax year because it coincides with the heaviest tipping and overtime of the calendar. A contemporaneous tip log, a verified Box 14 TIP and OT entry on the January 2027 W-2, and an accurate Schedule 1 deduction on the 2026 return convert holiday work into a measurable reduction in the 2027 Repayment Assistance Plan monthly payment of roughly $0.12 to $1.20 of annual RAP savings per dollar of AGI reduction, continuing across all twelve months of the 2027 payment cycle. The IRS April 2026 final regulations (T.D. 10046) settled the qualifying-occupation list and the tip-versus-service-charge distinction; the August 2026 overtime guidance (FS-2026-13) settled the FLSA half-premium carve-out. All that remains is the documentation.
