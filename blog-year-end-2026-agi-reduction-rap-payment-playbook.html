Year-End 2026 AGI Reduction Playbook for RAP Borrowers: The 10-Week Window Before December 31 That Decides Your 2027 Student Loan Payment

Ten and a half weeks separate today from December 31. For federal student loan borrowers on RAP or IBR, that window is the single highest-leverage AGI-planning window of the year. The AGI you lock in by year-end is the number the Education Department will pull from the IRS at your 2027 recertification, and it drives twelve months of monthly payments beginning next summer. Here are the eight levers that actually move AGI, the three that look like reducers but either do nothing or raise it, and the exact deadline that applies to each.

The Repayment Assistance Plan uses the Adjusted Gross Income line from your most recent federal tax return. For borrowers who enrolled during the first RAP enrollment wave in July, August, or September 2026, the current payment is set from the 2024 or 2025 AGI on file. The next recertification will land roughly twelve months after enrollment, so somewhere between July and September 2027. At that recertification, the servicer pulls the AGI from the 2026 return you file in April 2027. Every pre-tax dollar you move between now and December 31 reduces the AGI on that return. Every dollar you move in your IRA or HSA between January 1 and April 15, 2027 reduces it too, as long as you file after making the contribution.

The arithmetic of the RAP formula makes year-end planning worth the time. Under the current rules, each $1,000 reduction in AGI reduces the annual RAP payment obligation by $100 (10%), or roughly $8.33 a month. A borrower who moves $15,000 from after-tax take-home into pre-tax accounts between now and December 31 lowers next year's monthly payment by $125. Over the twelve months the new payment is in force, that is $1,500 of free cash flow — before accounting for the roughly $3,300 to $5,250 of federal income tax savings the pre-tax dollar also generates. For a PSLF-track borrower, the lower payment also reduces the amount paid toward principal across the next twelve months of qualifying payments, which pushes more balance into forgiveness at month 120. The compounded return on twenty minutes of year-end planning is unusually high.

How the Year-End Window Actually Works

Three different deadlines govern year-end AGI reduction, and most borrowers conflate them. Workplace retirement plan elective deferrals (401(k), 403(b), 457(b), SIMPLE IRA, Solo 401(k) employee portion) stop at December 31, 2026. The final pay period of 2026 is the last chance to withhold a dollar as a pre-tax deferral. HSA payroll contributions also stop at the final 2026 pay period, but outside-payroll HSA contributions for 2026 can be made as late as April 15, 2027 and still reduce 2026 AGI (deducted on Schedule 1, Line 13). Traditional IRA contributions follow the same April 15 cutoff. SEP-IRA contributions for self-employed borrowers can be made as late as the extended due date of the 2026 return (October 15, 2027). Knowing which lever extends past year-end and which one stops at the final paycheck is the difference between locking in a $125 monthly payment reduction and missing it entirely.

The Eight Year-End Moves That Actually Reduce AGI

1. Catch Up on the 401(k) or 403(b)

The 2026 elective deferral limit is $24,500 (IRS Notice 2025-82). Add $7,500 if you are age 50 or older, or $11,250 if you are between 60 and 63 under SECURE 2.0's expanded catch-up. If you have been contributing a flat percentage all year, check your October paystub against the limit. If you are under, most plans accept a one-time percentage override for the remaining pay periods of 2026. On a biweekly schedule, there are typically five or six pay periods left after October 19 — enough runway to add $5,000 to $15,000 if you reduce take-home temporarily. The deposit must land by December 31 for the deferral to count against 2026 AGI; a January payroll makeup does not reach back.

2. Fund the HSA Through December 31 (or Beyond)

The 2026 HSA limit is $4,400 for self-only HDHP coverage and $8,750 for family HDHP coverage (IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-19). Add $1,000 for the age-55 catch-up. HSA contributions made through payroll are a Section 125 cafeteria plan election: they come out of W-2 Box 1 and avoid both federal income tax and FICA, which is why they are the single most tax-efficient dollar most borrowers can save. If you did not elect the maximum at open enrollment, you can still make outside-payroll contributions directly to the HSA custodian until April 15, 2027. Outside-payroll contributions reduce AGI (Schedule 1, Line 13) but do not avoid FICA.

3. Traditional IRA Contribution

The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500, with a $1,000 age-50 catch-up. The deduction phases out based on your filing status and whether you are covered by a workplace retirement plan. For a single filer covered at work, the 2026 deduction phases out between $87,000 and $107,000 MAGI. For a joint filer where the taxpayer is covered at work, the phase-out is $140,000 to $160,000. If you have no workplace plan, there is no phase-out based on your own income. A $7,500 deductible contribution lowers 2026 AGI by $7,500, which trims the 2027 RAP payment by roughly $62.50 a month. The contribution window stays open until April 15, 2027.

4. Solo 401(k) Employee Deferral (1099 Borrowers Only)

If you have self-employment income — a side consulting gig, 1099 contractor work, a one-person LLC — a Solo 401(k) accepts the same $24,500 employee elective deferral as a workplace plan, plus an employer profit-sharing contribution up to 25% of net self-employment earnings for a combined 2026 cap of $70,000. Under SECURE 2.0 the plan can be ESTABLISHED as late as the extended return due date (October 15, 2027) and still accept employer profit-sharing for 2026, but the $24,500 employee deferral requires a written salary deferral election signed by December 31, 2026. If you want the full employee deferral, open the plan and sign the election this calendar year. The actual deposit can wait until the return is filed.

5. SEP-IRA (1099 Borrowers, No Dec 31 Deadline)

For self-employed borrowers who did not sign a Solo 401(k) deferral election by December 31, a SEP-IRA is the fallback. There is no establishment or election deadline during the tax year itself: the SEP can be opened and funded as late as the extended return due date (October 15, 2027). The contribution is 25% of net self-employment earnings up to $70,000 for 2026. A SEP contribution reduces AGI directly on Schedule 1 and does not reduce the self-employment tax base, so model both effects when sizing it.

6. SIMPLE IRA (Small Employer Participants)

If your employer offers a SIMPLE IRA, the 2026 employee deferral limit is $17,000 with a $3,500 age-50 catch-up. SIMPLE deferrals also stop at December 31 for a calendar-year plan.

7. Harvest Capital Losses

If you have appreciated or depreciated taxable brokerage positions, tax-loss harvesting trims the net capital gain line that flows into AGI. The 2026 ordinary-income offset is capped at $3,000 of net capital losses per year, with excess losses carried forward indefinitely. A $3,000 harvested loss trims AGI by $3,000, dropping the 2027 RAP payment by roughly $25 a month. This is a smaller lever than retirement contributions, but it stacks and the wash-sale rule is manageable with a replacement security. Settlement must occur by December 31; the trade date, not the settlement date, is what counts for 2026 tax reporting.

8. Dependent Care FSA Reimbursement Timing

If you elected a dependent care FSA at open enrollment last fall, the full $5,000 (or whatever you elected) is already reducing your W-2 Box 1 across 2026 pay periods. The year-end task is to spend down the balance by the plan's grace period or run-out deadline so you do not forfeit the deduction. The AGI reduction is baked in; losing the balance to use-it-or-lose-it does not increase AGI, but it wastes the dollars.

The Three Year-End Moves That Look Like AGI Reducers But Are Not

A. Charitable Giving

A cash donation to a qualified 501(c)(3) is an itemized deduction on Schedule A. It reduces TAXABLE income if you itemize — which, after the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act standard deduction, roughly 10% of filers do — but it does not change AGI. The RAP formula uses AGI, not taxable income, so a $5,000 charitable gift does not change your student loan payment. The one exception is a Qualified Charitable Distribution from an IRA for borrowers age 70-and-a-half or older, which transfers IRA funds directly to charity and is excluded from AGI up to $108,000 for 2026 (per IRS inflation adjustment). For a borrower using a Donor-Advised Fund to bunch deductions in alternate years, the tax savings are real but they do not change the RAP payment.

B. Roth 401(k) and Roth IRA Contributions

Roth contributions are post-tax. The dollar you contribute has already shown up in W-2 Box 1 wages or in Schedule 1 AGI. Nothing comes out of the AGI line as a result of the Roth contribution. If your employer offers both traditional and Roth 401(k) and your goal is to lower the RAP payment, choose traditional for the deferral portion. If you already made the Roth choice for 2026 and your employer plan accepts changes, you can usually switch the deferral type for the remaining pay periods of the year. Roth makes sense for long-term tax diversification; it does not help RAP.

C. Roth Conversions (These Raise AGI)

A Roth conversion moves pre-tax retirement dollars into a Roth account and the entire converted balance is added to that year's AGI as ordinary income. A $20,000 Roth conversion executed by December 31, 2026 raises your 2026 AGI by $20,000, which raises the 2027 RAP payment by roughly $167 a month. Many year-end tax articles frame Roth conversions as a smart move during a low-income year, and for a borrower who is NOT on an income-driven plan that logic holds. For a borrower on RAP or IBR, the AGI spike compounds in two directions: a higher monthly payment for the following twelve months, and — for PSLF borrowers — less balance forgiven at month 120. If you must do a Roth conversion while on RAP, do it in a year when you plan to make the standard-plan payment anyway.

Worked Example: $55,000 Projected 2026 AGI

Jordan, age 29, single, no dependents, is a RAP borrower with a $42,000 federal balance who enrolled in September 2026. Current payment: $266 a month (based on 2024 AGI of $48,000). Projected 2026 AGI with current withholding: $55,000. Jordan's recertification is scheduled for August 2027, which will pull the 2026 return.

Baseline 2027 RAP payment at $55,000 AGI: roughly $303 a month. Jordan runs the following year-end plan:

• Increase 401(k) elective deferral from 6% to 20% for the final six pay periods of 2026 ($55,000 / 26 pay periods × 14% × 6 pay periods = roughly $1,780 additional pre-tax).

• Fund HSA for $2,000 out of October through December paychecks (already on HDHP coverage).

• Make a $5,000 traditional IRA contribution before April 15, 2027 (Jordan is below the $87,000 phase-out and covered at work).

• Harvest $1,500 of net capital losses on a brokerage position.

Total AGI reduction: $10,280. 2026 AGI drops from $55,000 to $44,720. 2027 RAP payment drops from roughly $303 to roughly $217 a month — a $86 monthly reduction, or $1,032 of annual cash flow freed up. Federal income tax savings on the $10,280 (at 22% marginal): $2,262. Total year-one benefit: $3,294. Time investment: a one-page HR form plus two online transfers.

Worked Example: $95,000 Projected 2026 AGI

Priya, age 34, married filing jointly with one dependent, is a PSLF-track RAP borrower with a $118,000 federal balance. Current payment: $462 a month. Projected 2026 household AGI with current withholding: $95,000. Priya's recertification is scheduled for July 2027.

Baseline 2027 RAP payment at $95,000 AGI: roughly $700 a month. Priya runs the following year-end plan:

• Max out the 401(k) elective deferral for 2026 by overriding withholding on the last five paychecks (an additional $8,000 above baseline).

• Fund the family HSA to the $8,750 limit (already on HDHP; $2,100 of additional contribution for the remainder of 2026).

• Spouse contributes $7,500 to a non-deductible traditional IRA (phase-out limits the deduction). This does NOT reduce AGI and gets dropped from the plan.

Total AGI reduction: $10,100. 2026 household AGI drops from $95,000 to $84,900. 2027 RAP payment drops from roughly $700 to roughly $616 a month — an $84 monthly reduction, or $1,008 of annual cash flow freed up. For a PSLF-track borrower, the $1,008 of lower payments across the next twelve qualifying payment months is $1,008 of additional balance forgiven at month 120. Federal income tax savings on the $10,100 (at 22% marginal): $2,222. Total year-one benefit: $3,230 plus the PSLF compounding.

The 10-Week Action Timeline

Week of October 19 (now): Pull your most recent paystub. Calculate 2026 projected AGI from year-to-date W-2 Box 1 wages scaled to the full year. Log into StudentAid.gov and confirm the AGI the servicer currently has on file. Decide which levers you will pull.

Week of October 26: Submit 401(k) percentage change through your HR portal. Most changes take one or two pay periods to activate.

November 1 to November 30: Open enrollment runs at most private employers. See our open enrollment benefits guide for the 2027 benefits plan year decisions. Note that open enrollment is for the FOLLOWING year; current-year AGI reduction is still happening through payroll until December 31.

Mid-December: Verify your pay stub shows the increased 401(k) deferral. Request a one-time deferral increase on any discretionary year-end bonus. If you are self-employed, open the Solo 401(k) and sign the salary deferral election by December 31.

December 31: Final cutoff for 401(k), 403(b), 457(b), SIMPLE IRA, and Solo 401(k) employee deferral elections. Also the cutoff for tax-loss harvesting trades.

January through February 2027: Receive W-2 and verify Box 1 reflects all deferrals.

February through April 15, 2027: Final window to make 2026 contributions to a traditional IRA and to an HSA (outside payroll). File your 2026 return.

The RAP Formula Refresher

For borrowers who want to pressure-test their own numbers: under the current RAP formula, the annual payment obligation is 10% of AGI above the Federal Poverty Level protection, with a $10 monthly minimum and a 50% principal match on interest-only amounts. For 2026 the FPL for a single-person household is $15,650, with $5,580 added per additional household member. A single borrower at $55,000 AGI has $39,350 above FPL; 10% of that is $3,935; divided by 12 months is $328, before the dependent reduction and the interest waiver mechanics. For a full walk-through of the formula, see our RAP Calculator, which handles the tiered bands, the dependent deduction, and the interest waiver math. PSLF-track borrowers should run both the baseline and the reduced-AGI scenario through the PSLF tracker to see the forgiveness delta.

If you are still on a legacy plan (IBR, PAYE, or SAVE in forbearance) and considering the switch to RAP, run the plan comparison at both your current AGI and the reduced AGI you expect after year-end moves. The plan that is cheapest today may not be the plan that is cheapest at a lower AGI once dependent deductions and the RAP tier structure are applied. Common Year-End Mistakes

1. Waiting until the final pay period of 2026. Many 401(k) plans need one to two pay cycles to process a deferral change. If you file the change on December 20, you may miss the final paycheck of the year.

2. Confusing the IRA deadline (April 15) with the 401(k) deadline (December 31). Both windows are real; the IRA runway is longer, and borrowers often use the extra time poorly by assuming it applies to all accounts.

3. Chasing Roth conversions in a low-income year. This is standard tax-planning advice that actively hurts RAP borrowers. Model the AGI spike before pulling the trigger.

4. Over-electing dependent care FSA. If your projected daycare expense drops because a child aged out or started kindergarten, revise the election at open enrollment or risk forfeiting the unused balance.

5. Forgetting the IRA deduction phase-out. If you are covered by a workplace plan and above the phase-out, a traditional IRA contribution becomes non-deductible and does not reduce AGI. A non-deductible IRA still has a role in long-term planning (backdoor Roth, for example), but it does not help your RAP payment.

6. Treating charitable giving as an AGI reducer. Standard tax guides describe charitable gifts as a December 31 tax move. For RAP borrowers, cash gifts to charity are below the AGI line and do not change the monthly payment. QCDs for borrowers 70-and-a-half or older are the one exception.

Bottom Line

Ten and a half weeks separate October 19 from December 31. For RAP and IBR borrowers, that is the window to lock in a lower 2026 AGI, which becomes the lower 2027 RAP payment the servicer sets at your next recertification. The eight levers that actually work are: 401(k) or 403(b) catch-up, HSA funding, traditional IRA (through April 15), Solo 401(k) employee deferral (election by Dec 31), SEP-IRA (through October 15, 2027), SIMPLE IRA catch-up, tax-loss harvesting, and dependent care FSA utilization. The three year-end moves that look like reducers but are not: charitable giving, Roth 401(k)/Roth IRA, and Roth conversions. Twenty minutes of modeling this month produces twelve months of lower RAP payments beginning next summer; for PSLF borrowers, it also compounds into additional balance forgiven at month 120.
